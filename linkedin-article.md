# We Built a Flight Simulator for AI Capital Governance

**Every enterprise is spending millions on AI. Almost none can prove the money was well spent.**

---

I spent the last year asking CFOs, CIOs, and board directors one question:

*"Can you show me the economic constitution that governs your AI spending?"*

The answer, almost universally, was silence.

Not because they don't care. Because the tooling doesn't exist. We have budgets. We have dashboards. We have vendor contracts. But we don't have an **economic authority layer** — a system that makes every AI dollar prove its value, enforces stop conditions when it can't, and produces a board-grade evidence trail of every decision.

So we built one.

---

## What is CAPITAL//CONSTITUTION?

It's an interactive simulation — a flight simulator for AI capital governance — where you sit in the chair of the Capital Authority Officer for a fictional institution running five AI workloads worth $38.4M.

The portfolio is inside its annual budget. But it is **outside its economic constitution**. Three workloads are consuming capital without sufficient verified value.

Your job: make it defensible.

**Live demo:** [capital-constitution-demo.vercel.app](https://capital-constitution-demo.vercel.app)

## The Five Stations

### 1. Command Centre

You open to three executive orders waiting for your signature. A margin clock counts down to economic failure. An autonomous governor is already rerouting model traffic within its delegated authority. You see the entire portfolio — every workload, its run-rate, value-to-cost ratio, and economic state — in one view.

**Your action:** Issue the orders. Every one creates a sealed evidence receipt.

### 2. Capital Contracts

Each AI workload operates under an executable capital contract. Not a budget line — a binding instrument with six clauses: capital ceiling, value covenant, quality floor, model authority limit, automatic cure condition, and return-to-human-authority trigger.

**Your action:** A contract is in BREACH. Do you enforce the cure? Seal it? Terminate it? Every action changes the contract state and creates an auditable record.

### 3. Capital Market

Five workloads compete for $6.7M in reserve capital. Total bids: $9.2M. You can't fund everything.

**Your action:** Fund or reject each bid. A live budget bar tracks your allocation. You must make real tradeoffs — and you can't seal the auction until every bid is decided and you're within budget.

### 4. Margin Shock Laboratory

Six sliders let you stress-test what happens when users grow, premium model traffic spikes, retry rates explode, or pricing drops. Every slider has a real-time health indicator — green, amber, or red — anchored to the contract terms.

**Your action:** Drag Premium Model Share from 28% to 70%. Watch the margin collapse. Click Apply Cure to see the system's recommended recovery. Save the scenario as sealed evidence.

### 5. Evidence Record

Every decision you made — orders issued, contracts enforced, bids funded, cures applied — survives as an immutable-style evidence receipt with authority, evidence, action, risk accepted, quality boundary, and reconsideration terms.

**Your action:** Click "Test Defensibility" and GPT-5.6-luna analyses your entire evidence chain. It returns a defensibility verdict, identifies your strongest and weakest records, tells you the first question a regulator would ask, and produces a remediation plan with lessons learned. Save it as a PDF.

## Why a Flight Simulator?

Because governance is a skill. You can't learn it from a policy document.

Pilots don't learn to fly by reading the manual. They sit in a simulator and practice decisions under pressure — engine failures, crosswinds, instrument failures — until the right response is automatic.

AI capital governance is the same. The decisions are:

- **When do you cut a workload that users love but economics don't support?**
- **When do you protect funding for a workload the CEO wants to cut?**
- **When do you let the autonomous governor act, and when do you override it?**
- **When is "good enough" evidence actually good enough for a board?**

These are judgment calls. They require practice. CAPITAL//CONSTITUTION gives you a safe environment to make them, see the consequences, and build institutional muscle memory.

## The Governance Stack

What makes this different from a dashboard:

| Layer | Dashboard | CAPITAL//CONSTITUTION |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| Visibility | Shows you numbers | Shows you numbers |
| Authority | None | Every dollar has an owner, limit, and expiry |
| Enforcement | None | Automatic cures trigger when boundaries break |
| Evidence | None | Every decision is a sealed, auditable receipt |
| Accountability | Unclear | Return-to-human-authority clauses on every contract |
| Learning | None | Simulation debrief with lessons learned |

The difference is the word **constitution**. A budget tells you how much you can spend. A constitution tells you **under what conditions spending is legitimate** — and what happens when those conditions are violated.

## What We Learned Building It

**1. AI economics is a control problem, not a forecasting problem.**
You don't need a better forecast. You need stop conditions, cure protocols, and reversal triggers that activate automatically when reality diverges from plan.

**2. Evidence quality matters more than evidence quantity.**
A board doesn't need 400 metrics. It needs 6 receipts that prove authority was exercised, evidence was reviewed, risk was accepted knowingly, and reconsideration was scheduled.

**3. The autonomous governor is the hardest conversation.**
How much authority do you delegate to an AI system that manages AI spending? Level 3 (reroute model traffic up to $250k/month impact) was the boundary that generated the most debate.

**4. Reversibility is an underpriced asset.**
The portfolio constitution includes a rule: *"Reversible experiments receive preference when evidence quality is uncertain."* This single principle prevented three irreversible capital commitments in simulation.

**5. The export is the product.**
The board record PDF is what survives the meeting. If the simulation doesn't produce a document that a regulator would accept, the simulation failed.
